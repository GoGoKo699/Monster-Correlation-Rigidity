## 1. Main results and conventions

Work on the traceless Griess module, complexified when needed, with

$$d=196883,\qquad \kappa=13858/3,\qquad M=46/\sqrt{141},\qquad
\Delta=11161/13858.$$

Let $T$ be its symmetric multiplication tensor, $\tau=T/\sqrt{d\kappa}$, and

$$W|a\rangle=\kappa^{-1/2}\sum_{ij}T_{aij}|ij\rangle,\quad
P=WW^\dagger,\quad \sigma=P/d.$$

The ideal two-forward-use collective unitary test has rejection

$$\epsilon(U)=1-\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}\big[P U^{\otimes2}P(U^\dagger)^{\otimes2}\big].$$

Write

$$\|X\|_{2,d}:=\|X\|_F/\sqrt d,\qquad
D_{\mathbb M}(U)=\min_{\theta,g}\|U-e^{i\theta}\rho(g)\|_{2,d}.$$

The distance is normalized Frobenius distance, **not** operator or diamond distance. The gate model is a fixed unitary on the known logical subspace, with trusted probe preparation and measurement. No leakage, arbitrary channel noise, or adversarial variation between uses is included.

### Theorem 1: global-to-local gate certification

For every complex unitary $U\in U(d)$,

$$\boxed{\epsilon(U)\le10^{-12}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D_{\mathbb M}(U)\le\sqrt{\frac{1681\pi^2}{12528}}\sqrt{\epsilon(U)}
<1.151\sqrt{\epsilon(U)}.}$$

For every real orthogonal $O$, the same conclusion holds under the larger sufficient threshold $\epsilon(O)\le10^{-10}$.

There is no initial calibration or small-logarithm promise. The proof establishes such a neighborhood and then invokes the earlier local theorem. The complex threshold improves the earlier **sufficient** $10^{-25}$ threshold by a factor $10^{13}$. It is not an optimal threshold or a practical sample-complexity claim.

### Theorem 2: average transport rounds to one symmetry

Let $\mathcal A$ be the oriented normalized traceless Ising axes and define

$$\mathsf W(O)^2=\min_{s=\pm1,\,\pi\text{ bijective}}
\mathbb E_{a\in\mathcal A}\|sOa-\pi(a)\|^2.$$

Then

$$\boxed{\mathsf W(O)^2\le3\times10^{-9}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D_{\mathbb M}(O)^2\le
\frac{20842432}{196883}\,\mathsf W(O)^2
<106\,\mathsf W(O)^2.}$$

In particular, $D_{\mathbb M}(O)<10.3\,\mathsf W(O)$ in this regime. This is the missing implication from the preceding checkpoint: it produces proximity to **one group element**, not merely an arbitrary near-matching of the axis ensemble. The matching need not itself be an exact Monster permutation.

Both theorems are existential certificates. There is no efficient algorithm claimed for computing the matching, enumerating the axes, finding group words, or locating the nearest Monster element.

## 3. A global spectral comparison converts axis errors to involution errors

For $a\in\mathcal A$, put

$$\nu=\frac1{2\sqrt{141}},\qquad
J_a=I-2\Pi_\nu(L_a).$$

Here $\Pi_\nu$ is the orthogonal spectral projection for the eigenvalue $\nu$. By the Miyamoto construction, $J_a=\rho(t_a)$ for a genuine $2A$ involution $t_a$.

The smallest gap between $\nu$ and the other eigenvalues of $L_a$ is

$$g_*=\frac3{2\sqrt{141}}.$$

If symmetric matrices $A,B$ both have this prescribed spectrum (with arbitrary orientations), then

$$\|(I-2\Pi_\nu(A))-(I-2\Pi_\nu(B))\|_F^2
\le\frac4{g_*^2}\|A-B\|_F^2
=\frac{752}{3}\|A-B\|_F^2. \tag{3.1}$$

**Proof.** With spectral projections $P_\lambda,Q_\mu$,

$$\|A-B\|_F^2=\sum_{\lambda,\mu}(\lambda-\mu)^2
\operatorname{Tr}(P_\lambda Q_\mu),$$

while

$$\|\Pi_\nu(A)-\Pi_\nu(B)\|_F^2
=\sum_{\lambda,\mu}(1_{\lambda=\nu}-1_{\mu=\nu})^2
\operatorname{Tr}(P_\lambda Q_\mu).$$

Each term in the latter sum that is nonzero has $|\lambda-\mu|\ge g_*$. This proves (3.1) globally, without an operator-norm perturbation hypothesis. Multiplicities may be large but do not enter the coefficient.

For a chosen sign of $O$ and a chosen bijection $\pi$, define

$$z^2=\mathbb E_a\|O J_aO^T-J_{\pi(a)}\|_{2,d}^2.$$

The key contraction is

$$\mathbb E_a\|O L_aO^T-L_{Oa}\|_{2,d}^2
=\frac{\|O^{\otimes3}T-T\|_F^2}{d^2}
=\frac\kappa d\|O^{\otimes3}\tau-\tau\|^2. \tag{3.2}$$

To see the factors of $d$, the sum of squared residual slices over an orthonormal basis is the squared tensor norm. Averaging over the tight axis frame contributes $1/d$, and normalizing the matrix Frobenius norm contributes a second $1/d$.

Also,

$$\mathbb E_a\|L_{Oa}-L_{\pi(a)}\|_{2,d}^2
=\frac\kappa d\mathbb E_a\|Oa-\pi(a)\|^2.$$

Apply (3.1) and Minkowski's inequality in the Hilbert space of matrix-valued functions of $a$:

$$z^2\le\frac{752\kappa}{3d}
\left(\sqrt{2(1-\alpha(O))}
+\sqrt{\mathbb E_a\|Oa-\pi(a)\|^2}\right)^2. \tag{3.3}$$

For the sign and matching supplied by the average-transport theorem,

$$z^2\le B[1-|\alpha(O)|],\qquad
B=\frac{752\kappa}{3d}(\sqrt2+4\sqrt M)^2<508. \tag{3.4}$$

The diagnostic value is $B=507.2571232205866\ldots$. Thus

$$\boxed{z^2<1300\epsilon(O)} \tag{3.5}$$

for a real operation, and for the real approximation to an arbitrary complex $U$,

$$\boxed{z^2<170000\epsilon(U).} \tag{3.6}$$

These are mean errors. Some axes and assigned involutions may be poor matches. The next argument explicitly tolerates that.

## 4. Four conjugate generators imply a uniform spectral gap

Let $C$ be the $2A$ class, and let $\psi$ be any nontrivial irreducible complex representation of the Monster of degree $m$.

Choose four elements of $C$ that generate the group, repeating a generator if fewer suffice. If either the plus or minus eigenspace of $\psi(t)$ had dimension greater than $3m/4$, the corresponding four same-sign eigenspaces would have a nonzero common intersection. Every vector in that intersection spans an invariant line for all generators and hence for the group. This contradicts irreducibility unless $m=1$; the Monster has no nontrivial one-dimensional representations.

Both multiplicities are therefore at most $3m/4$, so

$$\boxed{\left|\frac{\chi_\psi(t)}{m}\right|\le\frac12.} \tag{4.1}$$

This is an elementary consequence of the published generation bound, not a new character-table calculation. It avoids needing all 194 irreducible character rows.

Uniform averaging over a conjugacy class acts by the scalar $\chi_\psi(t)/m$ on each irreducible representation. Consequently the uniform $C$ random walk has norm at most $1/2$ on functions orthogonal to constants. The same bound holds on the traceless part of the conjugation representation on $\operatorname{End}(V)$; its only invariant line is the scalar matrices.

## 5. A robust normalizer lemma

We first state the mechanism independently of the Monster.

**Lemma.** Let $G$ be finite, with a faithful absolutely irreducible real orthogonal representation $\rho:G\to O(d)$ and an involution conjugacy class $C$. Suppose uniform $C$ averaging has operator norm at most $q<1$ on every nontrivial irreducible representation. Let

$$s_0=\min_{g\ne h}\|\rho(g)-\rho(h)\|_{2,d}>0.$$

Given $O\in O(d)$, suppose elements $h_c\in G$ are assigned to the $c\in C$, with

$$\mathbb E_c\|O\rho(c)O^T-\rho(h_c)\|_{2,d}^2\le z^2.$$

Choose $\beta,t,L$ such that

$$z^2\le\beta t^2,\qquad
r:=\frac{q+\beta}{1-\beta}<1,\qquad
(|G|-1)r^L<1,\qquad
Lt<1,\qquad 3Lt<s_0.$$

Then there is an orthogonal normalizer $R$ of $\rho(G)$ such that

$$\min_{s=\pm1}\|O-sR\|_{2,d}^2\le\frac{z^2}{1-q}. \tag{5.1}$$

The assignments need not be a homomorphism, need not be injective, and need not remain in $C$.

### Proof step 1: discard a controlled fraction of bad generators

Call $c$ good if its assigned error is at most $t$. Markov's inequality shows that the bad fraction $\beta'\le\beta$.

The good-set walk is

$$T_{\rm good}=\frac{T_C-\beta'T_{\rm bad}}{1-\beta'}.$$

On the orthogonal complement of the constant functions its norm is at most

$$\frac{q+\beta'}{1-\beta'}\le r.$$

It is self-adjoint because every step is an involution, even though the good subset need not be conjugacy invariant.

If $n=|G|$, its $L$-step transition probabilities satisfy

$$\left|T_{\rm good}^L(e,g)-\frac1n\right|
\le\left(1-\frac1n\right)r^L<\frac1n.$$

Hence every group element is a product of exactly $L$ good class elements.

### Proof step 2: round conjugation on the entire group

For such a word $g=c_1\cdots c_L$, replacing its factors by the assigned $h_{c_j}$ yields some group element within $\delta:=Lt$ of $O\rho(g)O^T$. The product-error bound is a telescoping sum; all factors are orthogonal and the norm is bi-invariant.

Because $2\delta<s_0$, this nearby group element is unique and is the nearest group matrix. Call it $\rho(\phi(g))$.

For $g,h\in G$, the distance between $\rho(\phi(gh))$ and $\rho(\phi(g)\phi(h))$ is at most $3\delta<s_0$. They must coincide. If $\phi(g)=\phi(h)$, then the original conjugated matrices are at distance at most $2\delta<s_0$, so $g=h$. Thus $\phi$ is a group automorphism.

Importantly, for each original class element $c$, its actual nearest error under $\phi$ is no greater than its originally assigned error, even when $c$ was discarded as bad.

### Proof step 3: build an exact orthogonal intertwiner

Set

$$A=\mathbb E_g\rho(\phi(g))^T O\rho(g).$$

Every summand is within $\delta$ of $O$ in normalized Frobenius distance, so $\|A-O\|_{2,d}\le\delta<1=\|O\|_{2,d}$. Thus $A\ne0$.

Direct reindexing gives $A\rho(h)=\rho(\phi(h))A$. Absolute irreducibility implies $A^TA=a^2I$ for some $a>0$. Then $R=A/a$ is orthogonal and implements $\phi$ by conjugation.

### Proof step 4: recover a sharp average error bound

Let $Q=R^TO$. The nearest-error observation gives

$$\mathbb E_c\|Q\rho(c)-\rho(c)Q\|_{2,d}^2\le z^2.$$

The class-conjugation spectral bound implies

$$\mathbb E_c\|Q\rho(c)-\rho(c)Q\|_{2,d}^2
\ge2(1-q)\left(1-\left|\frac{\operatorname{Tr}Q}{d}\right|^2\right).$$

Since $Q$ is real orthogonal,

$$\min_{s=\pm1}\|Q-sI\|_{2,d}^2
=2\left(1-\left|\frac{\operatorname{Tr}Q}{d}\right|\right)
\le2\left(1-\left|\frac{\operatorname{Tr}Q}{d}\right|^2\right).$$

This proves (5.1). The group-word step supplies existence; the final average spectral estimate restores a square-root error bound without multiplying the final coefficient by $L$.

## 6. Specialize the normalizer lemma to the Monster

The retained degree-$d$ character row has maximum nonidentity value $4371$, so

$$s_0^2=2-\frac{2\cdot4371}{196883},\qquad s_0>5/4.$$

Use

$$q=1/2,\qquad\beta=1/16,\qquad t=1/600,\qquad L=250.$$

Then

$$r=3/5,\qquad\delta=5/12,\qquad3\delta=5/4<s_0,\qquad\delta<1.$$

For the exact Monster order

$$n=808017424794512875886459904961710757005754368000000000,$$

one has

$$(n-1)(3/5)^{250}=0.027876183988697\ldots<1.$$

All these inequalities are checked with exact rational arithmetic. We obtain an orthogonal normalizer $R$ whenever

$$z^2\le\beta t^2=\frac1{5760000}=1.7361111\ldots\times10^{-7}. \tag{6.1}$$

### Why an orthogonal normalizer is already a Monster action, up to sign

This last step is **not automatic for a general finite group**.

If $R$ normalizes $\rho(\mathbb M)$, it preserves the invariant subspace of $V^{\otimes3}$. That subspace is the one-dimensional real line spanned by $T$, so $R^{\otimes3}T=\pm T$. Replace $R$ by $-R$ when necessary; because the tensor has odd degree, the new $R$ fixes $T$. It also preserves the real metric. Extending it to fix the Griess identity direction makes it an automorphism of the full algebra. By the full automorphism theorem, it belongs to $\rho(\mathbb M)$.

Thus

$$N_{O(d)}(\rho(\mathbb M))=\{\pm\rho(g):g\in\mathbb M\}.$$

No appeal to a classification of outer automorphisms of the Monster is required.

The lemma therefore proves

$$\boxed{z^2\le1/5760000\implies D_{\mathbb M}(O)^2\le2z^2.} \tag{6.2}$$

The small $A_5$ negative control in the numerical checker demonstrates why the invariant-tensor step must not be omitted: an odd permutation normalizes its four-dimensional $A_5$ action but is outside phase times that group.

## 7. Prove the gate thresholds and restore the local prefactor

For real $O$ with $\epsilon(O)\le10^{-10}$, (3.5) gives

$$z^2<1.3\times10^{-7}<1/5760000.$$

Thus

$$D_{\mathbb M}(O)\le\sqrt{2600}\sqrt{\epsilon(O)}<52\sqrt{\epsilon(O)}. \tag{7.1}$$

For a complex $U$ with $\epsilon(U)\le10^{-12}$, (3.6) gives

$$z^2<1.7\times10^{-7}<1/5760000.$$

Combining the real reduction with (6.2),

$$D_{\mathbb M}(U)
\le(\sqrt{K_R}+\sqrt{340000})\sqrt{\epsilon(U)}
<600\sqrt{\epsilon(U)}. \tag{7.2}$$

At the claimed thresholds, these **preliminary** normalized bounds imply an ordinary operator-norm distance below $1/3$ from a phase times a Monster element:

$$\sqrt d\,52\sqrt{10^{-10}}<1/3,\qquad
\sqrt d\,600\sqrt{10^{-12}}<1/3.$$

If a unitary $X$ satisfies $\|X-I\|_{\rm op}\le1/3$, its principal Hermitian logarithm $K$ has norm at most $2\arcsin(1/6)$. The centered logarithm

$$H=K-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}K}{d}I$$

has norm at most $4\arcsin(1/6)<\pi/4$. Absorb the subtracted trace into the global phase. The earlier local pair-test theorem now applies and improves (7.1–7.2) to the coefficient in Theorem 1.

For an elementary enclosure, $4\arcsin(1/6)\le4/\sqrt{35}<\pi/4$, using $\pi>3$. The displayed $1.151$ coefficient follows from $\pi<355/113$ and an exact rational inequality.

## 8. Prove the direct transport-to-group result

Choose the sign and bijection attaining $w=\mathsf W(O)$; do not assume this sign maximizes $\alpha$.

The earlier pointwise upper-deficit estimate gives

$$M-f(Oa)\le2M\|Oa-\pi(a)\|^2.$$

Averaging and using the axis third moment yields

$$1-\alpha(O)\le2w^2,\qquad
\|O^{\otimes3}\tau-\tau\|^2\le4w^2.$$

Therefore (3.3) gives

$$z^2\le9\frac{752\kappa}{3d}w^2
=\frac{10421216}{196883}w^2=:A_Ww^2.$$

The constant is $A_W=52.9310097875\ldots$. When $w^2\le3\times10^{-9}$, (6.1) holds. Equation (6.2) gives

$$D_{\mathbb M}(O)^2\le2A_Ww^2<106w^2.$$

This establishes Theorem 2. It does not assert that the optimizing bijection equals the action of the nearby Monster element on every axis.

## 9. Interpretation, statistical limits, and nonclaims

The new chain is

$$\text{small quantum-test loss}
\to\text{small average axis/multiplication error}
\to\text{small average involution-conjugation error}
\to\text{an exact group automorphism}
\to\text{a nearby single Monster symmetry}.$$

A fixed large fraction of good involutions is enough. The argument does not round every axis pair or require a uniformly accurate matching. The $250$ steps are an existence argument inside the proof; they are **not** additional queries to the tested quantum gate.

A zero-rejection independent sample would need about $-\log(0.05)/10^{-12}\approx3\times10^{12}$ trials to put its 95% upper binomial confidence bound below the complex threshold. This is vastly less than the preceding bound's $3\times10^{25}$ trials but still impractical. It is a limitation of this sufficient certificate, not a lower bound on all Monster tests.

The state and projector still act on large logical registers. No efficient probe preparation, projector measurement, nearest-group algorithm, fault-tolerant implementation, speedup, or physical advantage has been proved. An 18-qubit logical register or a two-query experiment description does not supply any of those missing resources.

The result is not claimed to be a first theorem in symmetry testing or approximate representation theory. Relevant neighboring frameworks include quantum symmetry tests [S6] and Hilbert–Schmidt stability of approximate representations [S7]. This proof starts from a single correlation-preservation loss and must create a nearby map into a **fixed** finite matrix group; the relationship to known stability and almost-normalizer theorems requires a fuller audit. Searches did not establish priority. Norton's original chapter remains unread in this project; we use his trace identities through Matsuo.
