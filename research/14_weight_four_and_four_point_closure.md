## 1. Result, and why the next question changes

Keep the physical-selection class of note 12: nontrivial simple unitary strongly rational bosonic holomorphic chiral CFTs, without weight-one currents, minimizing positive central charge. In this restricted class, earlier source theorems give c=24, character J and the primary multiplication identities. No Monster action, character decomposition, known multiplication table or axis system is an assumption.

Let S(a,b) be the weight-four primary part of a_(-1)b for primary weight-two a,b. With the symmetric-tensor convention below,

$$S^\dagger S=54\Pi_{s+},\qquad SS^\dagger=54I_{P_4},\qquad \dim P_4=842609326.$$

Thus every weight-four primary is generated by such pairs, with a fixed total squared coupling. However, this channel again imposes no additional constraint after the existing multiplication identities.

The more consequential result is a **conditional four-point closure statement**. For any real symmetric primary cubic satisfying the full joint operator identities from note 12, the entire four-point kernel of external weight-two primaries lies in a six-dimensional commutative operator algebra, with universal scalar eigenfunctions. Ordinary crossing and all its Virasoro-block Gram-positivity/rank tests are already compatible with those identities. Positivity at all weights follows by comparison with the known unitary realization, not by an infinite numerical test. This does not say the component correlators are identical, nor that the cubic is unique, nor that every such abstract cubic extends to a CFT.

It is therefore not productive to keep treating each next intermediate weight of this same four-point function as a potentially independent selection principle. The next bounded object should involve compatibility of **mixed interactions**, for example the action of a weight-two field on two weight-three primary fields. That tests consistency of shared intermediate states, not just their summed production norms.

## 2. Conventions and necessary inputs

Use the positive Hermitian/PCT conventions of note 13 and [DL], real on PCT-fixed states. Put D=L_-1, a*b=a_(1)b on full V2, g_ab=<a,b>, and

$$a*b=\mu(a,b)+\frac{g_{ab}}6\omega,\qquad \|\omega\|^2=12.$$

The primary operators L_a b=mu(a,b) are symmetric and traceless. In an orthonormal primary basis, set

$$F(X)=\sum_i L_iXL_i,\quad m(X)=\sum_{ij}X_{ij}\mu(e_i,e_j),\quad Q=m^\dagger m,\quad J(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)I.$$

T denotes transposition of a matrix, equivalently swap on P2 tensor P2. The **joint identities**, not just the numerical spectrum of an unrelated F, are used:

* m m^dagger=kappa I, kappa=13858/3, hence Q^2=kappa Q;
* F(I)=kappa I, FQ=899Q, QJ=0, J^2=dJ, d=196883;
* F commutes with T; Q and J have symmetric range;
* on the symmetric complement of I and im Q, F has roots 155/3,-7/3; on the antisymmetric space, its roots are 845/3,-1/3.

These are the note-12 consequences of [H]'s conformal-design replacement for [M]'s trace hypotheses. They are not assumed Monster representation theory. Their multiplicities are determined by trace equations as in note 12. The present note does not independently reprove the imported conformal-design and trace theorems.

Use a⊙b=(a tensor b+b tensor a)/2, regarded as a symmetric matrix with its Frobenius metric. Thus an orthonormal symmetric basis is e_i tensor e_i and (e_i tensor e_j+e_j tensor e_i)/sqrt(2) for i<j. S(a⊙b)=S(a,b), so this latter basis maps to sqrt(2)S(e_i,e_j). Consequently SS^dagger sums over **all ordered pairs**; equivalently diagonal terms have weight one and unordered off-diagonal terms weight two. This differs from note 13's unit exterior basis convention.

## 3. Subtract all descendants

The raw x=a_(-1)b has weight four. In the c=24 unitary theory,

$$V_4=(V_\omega)_4\ \perp\ (L_{-2}P_2+D^2P_2)\ \perp\ DP_3\ \perp\ P_4.$$

The middle two descendants of the same primary are not orthogonal. Their Gram matrix and the vacuum Gram matrix are

$$G_2=\begin{pmatrix}20&12\\12&40\end{pmatrix},\qquad
G_0=\begin{pmatrix}120&72\\72&384\end{pmatrix},$$

in bases (L_-2 u,D^2 u) for unit u in P2 and (L_-4 1,L_-2^2 1), respectively. Their determinants are 656 and 40896. These follow from the Virasoro commutators [M, section 1].

Writing B(a,b)=a_(0)b-(1/2)D(a*b), direct commutation gives

$$L_1x=3B(a,b)+\tfrac32D(a*b),\quad L_2x=4(a*b),\quad L_3x=0,\quad L_4x=6g_{ab}1.$$

The overlap vector with the P2 level-two descendants is (4,6) times the coefficient mu(a,b); the vacuum overlap vector is g_ab(6,8). Also <DB,DB'>=6<B,B'> and the coefficient of DB is 1/2. Solving the two Gram systems gives

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
S(a,b)={}&a_{(-1)}b-\tfrac12DB(a,b)
-\frac{11L_{-2}+9D^2}{82}\mu(a,b)\\
&-g_{ab}\left(\frac3{71}L_{-4}1+\frac{11}{852}L_{-2}^21\right).
\end{aligned}}\tag{1}$$

All L_m, 1<=m<=4, annihilate this state; larger m vanish by degree. Skew symmetry gives a_(-1)b-b_(-1)a=DB(a,b), so S is symmetric. The raw product is generally not symmetric. Formula (1) includes both vacuum descendants, both P2 level-two descendants, and the P3 derivative. Treating only D^2 mu as the level-two subtraction gives the wrong projection.

For the small free-boson controls, the corresponding general-c coefficients are

$$\frac{22}{5c+44},\quad\frac{3c}{4(5c+44)},\quad
\frac6{5c+22},\quad\frac{44}{c(5c+22)},$$

in the order L_-2 mu, D^2 mu, L_-4 1 and L_-2^2 1. This is a derivation from the general Gram matrices, not a claim that those examples satisfy the extremal c=24 assumptions.

## 4. Uncontracted four-label Gram form

For four independent primaries a,b,c,d, put X=<a*c,b*d>, Y=<a*b,c*d>, Z=<a*d,b*c>. Mode adjoints, the commutator [a_(3),c_(-1)], and note 13 give

$$\langle a_{(-1)}b,c_{(-1)}d\rangle
=g_{ac}g_{bd}+g_{ad}g_{bc}+2X+Y-Z.\tag{2}$$

The derivative B contribution is (3/2)(X-Z). Removing it leaves a symmetric form with contraction part (X+Z)/2+Y. The P2 descendant contribution is (49/41)<mu(a,b),mu(c,d)>; the vacuum contribution is (76/213)g_ab g_cd. Thus

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\langle S(a,b),S(c,d)\rangle={}&\frac76(g_{ac}g_{bd}+g_{ad}g_{bc})\\
&+\tfrac12\big(\langle\mu(a,c),\mu(b,d)\rangle+\langle\mu(a,d),\mu(b,c)\rangle\big)\\
&-\frac8{41}\langle\mu(a,b),\mu(c,d)\rangle-\frac5{213}g_{ab}g_{cd}.
\end{aligned}}\tag{3}$$

On Sym^2 P2 this is precisely

$$\boxed{S^\dagger S=F_++\tfrac73 I-\tfrac8{41}Q-\tfrac5{213}J.}\tag{4}$$

Its eigenvalues on the scalar, multiplication, positive-complement and negative-complement sectors are respectively 0,0,54,0. In particular the scalar cancellation uses d=196883 and is not an arbitrary normalization adjustment.

The character and the nondegenerate descendant Gram matrices give

$$\dim P_4=864299970-2-2(196883)-21296876=842609326.$$

The right side of (4) has exactly this rank. Since the inner product is positive, rank S=rank S^dagger S, proving onto generation and SS^dagger=54I. If E_ij,A=<S(e_i,e_j),f_A> in a real orthonormal primary basis, then

$$\sum_{i,j}E_{ij,A}E_{ij,B}=54\delta_{AB},\qquad
\sum_{i,j,A}E_{ij,A}^2=45500903604.$$

These are dimensionless OPE-strength identities, not particle production rates or access probabilities. An abstract realization sqrt(54)Pi_s+ already supplies the same Gram data once the joint multiplication identities hold. As at weight three, the factor is unique only up to an output orthogonal transformation. A complete VOA has not been constructed this way.

## 5. Sum every intermediate level instead of continuing one level at a time

For n>=0 let M_n(a tensor b)=a_(3-n)b in V_n, including descendants. Define the reflected four-point kernel by the formal expansion

$$K_{ab,cd}(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\langle M_n(a,b),M_n(c,d)\rangle z^{n-4}.
$$

In a VOA it is the corresponding expansion of <b,Y(a,1)Y(c,z)d>. Put

A=g_ab g_cd, B_0=g_ac g_bd, C_0=g_ad g_bc,

and retain X,Y,Z for the FULL product contractions above. Then

$$\boxed{K_{ab,cd}(z)=\frac{A}{z^4}+\frac{B_0}{(1-z)^4}+C_0
+\frac{Y}{z^2}+\frac{X}{(1-z)^2}
+\frac{Y+X-Z}{z(1-z)}.}\tag{5}$$

This is not a truncation. For n>=4 the field adjoint a_(3-n)^dagger=a_(n-1) gives

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle M_n(a,b),M_n(c,d)\rangle
={}&\binom{n-1}{3}g_{ac}g_{bd}+\delta_{n,4}g_{ad}g_{bc}\\
&+(n-2)X+Y-Z.\tag{6}
\end{aligned}$$

Indeed [a_(n-1),c_(3-n)] equals B(a,c)_(2)+(n-2)(a*c)_(1)+binom(n-1,3)g_ac I. Its remaining matrix element is <b,B(a,c)_(2)d>=Y-Z by note 13. The noncommutator term survives only at n=4. At n=0,1,2,3 the coefficients are A,0,Y,Y+X-Z. Summing these exact coefficients proves (5), using only the mode identities. Rational genus-zero reconstruction from the poles is a standard alternative; [T] uses the related contracted genus-zero correlators. No new general rationality theorem is claimed.

Under z->1-z, the scalar structures exchange A<->B_0 and X<->Y. Under z->z/(z-1), they exchange B_0<->C_0 and X<->Z, with conformal factor (1-z)^(-4). Formula (5) obeys both identities algebraically. The symmetries of a real symmetric multiplication tensor ensure the required external-label permutations. Ordinary crossing of this rational four-point function does not fix the unknown cubic.

## 6. A precise conditional closure theorem for four-point positivity

On the ORDERED tensor square (not just Sym^2), set s^2=1/3. Formula (5) becomes

$$\begin{aligned}
K(z)={}&\frac J{z^4}+\frac I{(1-z)^4}+T
+\frac{Q+s^2J}{z^2}+\frac{F+s^2I}{(1-z)^2}\\
&+\frac{Q+F-FT+s^2(J+I-T)}{z(1-z)}.\tag{7}
\end{aligned}$$

The operators I,T,F,FT,Q,J commute under the inputs in section 2. Their joint eigenspaces, with no group-theoretic labels, are

| Sector | T | F | Q | J | Dimension |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Scalar | 1 | 13858/3 | 0 | 196883 | 1 |
| Multiplication | 1 | 899 | 13858/3 | 0 | 196883 |
| Symmetric positive | 1 | 155/3 | 0 | 0 | 842609326 |
| Symmetric negative | 1 | -7/3 | 0 | 0 | 18538750076 |
| Antisymmetric positive | -1 | 845/3 | 0 | 0 | 21296876 |
| Antisymmetric negative | -1 | -1/3 | 0 | 0 | 19360062527 |

**Proposition (conditional closure).** Suppose a real symmetric cubic on the d-dimensional positive-metric space satisfies ALL the joint identities and trace multiplicities in section 2. Assign its first-primary four-point kernel by (7). Then:

1. the scalar rational eigenfunction in each of these six sectors is universal;
2. its Virasoro-primary Gram coefficients at every intermediate weight have the same six scalar eigenvalues as in any existing unitary realization;
3. their positivity and rank bounds from the J-character primary multiplicities follow from the known unitary realization. They add no extra condition beyond the specified algebraic system at this level of four-point testing.

**Proof.** Substituting each row of the table into (7) gives its scalar eigenfunction. At central charge 24, Virasoro Ward identities fix the descendant contribution of each primary of weight h as a scalar series f_h(z)=z^(h-4)(1+...), with the vacuum null derivative removed. Successively subtracting these universal series from K(z) is a triangular recursion. Therefore every primary Gram operator A_h is a linear combination of the same six orthogonal projectors, with scalar coefficients independent of the candidate cubic. Use the known unitary moonshine theory only as a witness that these scalar coefficients are nonnegative and their positive-rank sums do not exceed the available primary multiplicities. Projector ranks and J-character multiplicities are the same for the abstract candidate. This transfers the positivity/rank inequalities, proving the proposition.

This reference argument does not insert the known multiplication table or automorphism group into the candidate. It is analogous to using a known solution to establish existence of positive scalar numbers, not to identify an unknown tensor. No infinite set of conformal-block coefficients has been numerically checked here. Completeness of the underlying Virasoro decomposition and its fixed descendant coefficients are standard unitary CFT inputs [DL, M, T].

For normalization comparison, the ordered primary Gram coefficients at weights two, three and four are Q, 564Pi_a+ and 54Pi_s+, respectively. The 564 is twice note 13's exterior value282: M_3 on a normalized antisymmetric tensor gives sqrt(2)B(a,b). This distinction is essential.

**What the proposition does not prove.** Different candidate cubics could yield different component correlators through differently embedded projectors. An arbitrary isometry of the pair space is not necessarily O tensor O for a field-basis change O. No pair of inequivalent cubics satisfying the full identities has been exhibited. Those identities themselves might already characterize the desired algebra, but that classification is not established here. Nor does the proposition assign a common set of all higher OPEs satisfying mixed correlator factorization. Positivity of one family of four-point functions is not sufficient to construct a complete local CFT.

## 7. Prior generation result and the first mixed target

A pertinent source found in this continuation is [T, Theorems 3.3-3.4]: for a VOA with V1=0, nonzero P2, completely reducible Virasoro structure and nondegenerate invariant form, a specified weight-six quadratic Casimir lying in the vacuum Virasoro module yields a differential equation and generation by V2. The source explicitly discusses the moonshine case and the weight-three antisymmetric decomposition. Generation by the first level must therefore not be advertised as a new general phenomenon.

For the extremal class, the conformal-design trace condition supplies this Casimir membership: pairing sum_i e_i(-3)e_i with a weight-six vector reduces to its zero-mode trace on P2. It vanishes on the complement of the vacuum module. The omitted stress-direction trace is also zero there by Virasoro invariance. Nondegeneracy then places the Casimir in the vacuum module. This is the same design-to-Casimir mechanism used by [H,M]. The cited theorem is a prior, stronger *iterated generation* statement; it does not classify the multiplication or equate iterated generation with a one-step pair map at every level. The note's constants54 and282 address normalized individual channels.

The next bounded object is the pullback of a weight-two zero mode onto the generated P3 channel:

$$T_e(x,y)=\langle Bx,e_{(1)}By\rangle,\qquad x,y\in\wedge^2P_2.$$

The compression to primary P3 is implicit between B^dagger and B; e_(1) need not preserve primarity before that compression. Since B kills ker Pi_a+, any valid pullback must obey

$$T_e=\Pi_{a+}T_e\Pi_{a+},\qquad T_e^\dagger=T_e$$

for real e. These concern five external weight-two labels and shared intermediate states. [M, equation (2.2) and section 2.2] explicitly introduces an antisymmetric five-linear form because the general five-field expression is not reducible to the binary product and metric alone. Its exceptional fifth-trace identity may in turn constrain that form. The next work must determine this, not assume the five-form is free or already fixed. A new kernel-factorization identity, or a rigorous demonstration of its redundancy, is the target. Simply computing the next coefficient of (7) is not.
