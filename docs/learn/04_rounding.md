# 4. From nearly extremal to exact

[Previous](03_ising.md) · [Learning path](README.md) · [Next](05_worked_certificate.md)

**Question:** how can inequalities with finite errors imply an exact algebraic conclusion?

This lesson follows [core Q1–Q4](../../research/uniform_extraction_core.md). No additional chapter of Gaberdiel is needed. Yamauchi Section 4, Theorem 4.6, is the secondary guide to the discrete pair structure; the proof's primary citation is Sakuma's Theorem 4.4.

## First control the field direction

The loss of a unit primary $x$ is $M-f(x)$. Near each exact maximizing direction $a$, the core proves

```math
M-f(x)\ge 2\|x-a\|^2
\quad\text{when}\quad \|x-a\|\le\frac1{10}.
```

A useful mental picture is a peak whose height decreases at least quadratically as one moves away from its summit. The function being plotted is a self-coupling on a space of field directions, not a physical potential in spacetime.

The local estimate comes from the allowed weight-two eigenvalues of an internal Ising stress tensor: $0$, $1/16$, $1/2$, and $2$. The core converts this spectral information into a uniform bound on the cubic's tangent coefficients. It does not need the multiplicities of these eigenspaces or a Monster multiplication table.

But the displayed estimate is **local**. On its own, it says nothing about a point far away. We must not assume the very proximity we are trying to prove.

## Why a high value must enter one of those neighborhoods

There are two ingredients: the local quadratic loss and the gap between $M$ and every other stationary value. Each primary sphere is compact because it is finite dimensional.

Here is the logic of the global step. Once an input exceeds the lower stationary-value cap, the actual global maximum cannot be a lower stationary point, so a maximizing direction with value $M$ exists. Consider the region outside radius-$1/10$ neighborhoods of all such directions. The maximum over this remaining compact region occurs either on a neighborhood boundary or at an interior stationary point. Boundary points have loss at least $2(1/10)^2=1/50$. Interior stationary points have loss at least $M-M_2>1/2$. Therefore an input with smaller loss than $1/50$ cannot lie in that region.

The local estimate now applies, giving

```math
f(x)\ge M-\epsilon,\quad 0\le\epsilon<\frac1{50}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\|x-a\|\le\sqrt{\epsilon/2}
```

for some exact maximizing direction $a$. The core also supplies the separation and finiteness details used in this compactness argument.

The same constants apply in every admissible candidate. We are not assuming the collection of all VOAs is compact, running gradient ascent, or providing an algorithm that finds $a$.

## Then control the pair

Apply the bound separately to the supplied fields $x,y$. For deficits $\epsilon_x,\epsilon_y$, obtain exact directions $a,b$ with

```math
\|x-a\|\le r_x=\sqrt{\epsilon_x/2},\qquad
\|y-b\|\le r_y=\sqrt{\epsilon_y/2}.
```

The elementary identity

```math
\langle a,b\rangle-\langle x,y\rangle
=\langle a-x,b\rangle+\langle x,b-y\rangle
```

and unit norms imply an overlap error at most $r_x+r_y$. No additional product $r_xr_y$ is needed with this decomposition.

Suppose also $|\langle x,y\rangle+1/47|\le\delta$. Then

```math
\left|\langle a,b\rangle+\frac1{47}\right|
\le\delta+r_x+r_y.
```

This is ordinary error propagation. The discrete classification of Ising-pair overlaps has not yet been used.

## The discrete step

Sakuma's theorem permits only a finite set of overlaps for exact Ising vectors in the relevant positive-real setting. Zero is allowed, and the smallest positive overlap is $1/256$. There are no negative possibilities. Thus

```math
|\langle e_a,e_b\rangle|<\frac1{256}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\langle e_a,e_b\rangle=0.
```

For comparison, an integer $n$ with $|n|<1$ must be zero. The inequality supplies precision; the discrete allowed set supplies exactness. Here discreteness comes from an imported theorem, not from rounding a numerical output by convention.

Using the stress-to-primary conversion from Lesson 3, a sufficient combined budget is

```math
\boxed{\delta+\sqrt{\epsilon_x/2}+\sqrt{\epsilon_y/2}<\frac3{188}.}
```

The inequality is strict because the boundary corresponds to the first nonzero allowed Ising overlap. Orthogonality gives commuting Ising subalgebras, with vanishing singular mutual OPE. It does not mean all regular products vanish.

## The exact endpoint belongs to prior work

Abe–Lam–Yamada's Theorem A.1 identifies the **ambient VOA** as moonshine from an orthogonal Ising pair in its stated class. It does not merely identify the subalgebra generated by the pair. The full exact ambient assumptions are given on the [assumptions page](assumptions_and_sources.md).

The project supplies a sufficient quantitative route into that theorem. It does not prove bare uniqueness of the ambient class, construct the isomorphism, or establish the axioms from measurements.

**2.** What makes the final overlap exactly zero? A: small numerical error alone. B: small error together with the discrete allowed overlap set.
